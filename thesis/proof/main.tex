\begin{abstract}
   This is an example problem illustrating that training an imputation network with maximum likelihood estimation on Bernoulli random missingness won't converge to the correct solution when the capacity of the network is limited; i.e., the imputation network won't generalise well to different patterns of missingness. Instead, I propose an alternative training objective to maximum likelihood that encourages good generalisation across all patterns of missingness even when the network capacity is limited.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup} 

Suppose we want to transmit a 2-bit message $Y \in \{A,B\}^2$ through a 1-bit channel $Z$. Below is a table recording the probability of every possible message. The entry $P_{ij}$ records the probability $P(Y_1 = i, Y_2 = j)$.

\begin{tabular}{c c c}
   & A & B \\
   A & 0.4 & 0.1 \\
   B & 0.2 & 0.3
\end{tabular}

Additionally, suppose that the message always has 1 missing bit (not necessarily the same one). Below is a table recording the conditional probability of the full message $Y$ given a partial observation $X \in \{A,B,?\}^2$ controlled by the missingness pattern $M \in \{0,1\}$.

\begin{tabular}{c c c c}
   $M$ & $X$ & $Y$ & $P(Y | X)$ \\ \hline
   $0$ & $(A,?)$ & $(A,A)$ & 0.8 \\
    &  & $(A,B)$ & 0.2 \\
    & $(B,?)$ & $(B,A)$ & 0.4 \\
    & & $(B,B)$ & 0.6 \\
   $1$ & $(?,A)$ & $(A,A)$ & 0.67 \\
    &  & $(B,A)$ & 0.33 \\
    & $(?,B)$ & $(A,B)$ & 0.25 \\
    & & $(B,B)$ & 0.75 \\
\end{tabular}

We assume that $M$ and $Y$ are independent (missing at random), but we make no other assumptions about $P(M)$ at this stage.

If we had a 2-bit channel $Z$, we could perfectly transmit any partial message:

$$Z(X,M) = \left\{
\begin{array}{ll}
   (M, X_1), & \textrm{if } M=0 \\
   (M, X_2), & \textrm{if } M=1
\end{array} \right.$$

Unfortunately, $Z$ only has 1 bit.

\section{Conventional approach} 

We want to find a $Z$ that maximises the likelihood of $Y$. Firstly, we have to assume a distribution on $M$. Assume that $P(M=1) = 0.5$. 

\begin{tabular}{c c}
   $X$ & $P(X)$ \\ \hline
   $P(A,?)$ & 0.25 \\
   $P(B,?)$ & 0.25 \\
   $P(?,A)$ & 0.3 \\
   $P(?,B)$ & 0.2 
\end{tabular}

The entropy of the partial message is $H(X) = - \sum_{x} P(X=x) \log P(X=x) = \frac{1}{10} (13 + 5 \log_2 5 - 3 \log_2 3)$.

We want $Z$ to maximise the information gain $\mathbb{E}_{P(Y,M,Z)} \log P(Y | Z)$:

\begin{align*}
   \mathbb{E}_{P(Y,M,Z)} \log P(Y | Z) &= \mathbb{E}_{P(Z | Y,M) P(Y) P(M)} \log P(Y | Z) \\
   &= \mathbb{E}_{P(Z | Y,M) P(Y) P(M)} \log \sum_{X} P(Y | X) P(X | Z) \\
\end{align*}
