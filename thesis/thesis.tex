%% Abstract:
%%
\abstract{This dissertation explores the problems inherent in applying deep learning algorithms to groups of data. My claim is that groups should be represented as random variables whose values should be inferred from data. This approach has the potential to unlock solutions in many important domains of machine learning, including disentangling the generative factors of data, performing missing data imputation, or training robust predictors. However, grouped data also comes with challenges, especially when the data is high-dimensional and non-linear. Addressing these limitations is the focus of the technical contributions of my doctorate.}

\chapter{Conclusion}

This thesis has explored the application of group-level representations inferred through Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) to three key problems in machine learning: style-content disentanglement, missing data imputation, and unsupervised domain adaptation. 

We first introduced the Context-Aware Variational Autoencoder (CxVAE), a novel representation learning method that can disentangle style and content factors in datasets with conditional shift. CxVAE outperforms existing methods by explicitly enforcing independence across content variables inferred from each environment. This contribution highlights the power of group-level representations in addressing complex relationships within data.

We then addressed the challenge of missing data imputation by proposing the Multiple-Instance Conditional Variational Autoencoder (MICVAE). This method leverages a group-level latent variable to capture the content within incomplete observations, generalizing well to unseen patterns of missingness. 

Finally, we explored the application of group-level representations to unsupervised domain adaptation. We introduced the concept of ``contextual predictors'' that utilize the group-level latent variable to achieve superior performance compared to traditional methods relying solely on individual observations. 

These findings demonstrate the versatility and effectiveness of group-level representations inferred through VAEs in tackling diverse tasks within probabilistic machine learning. This paves the way for further exploration of group-level representations in various applications, potentially leading to significant advancements in areas like interpretable machine learning and generalizable learning across different data environments. 

Future work could explore the application of these methods to even more complex data structures and investigate the potential for combining them with other deep learning architectures for even more powerful representations.
