### Evaluation metric

In the end, I decided not to use the DCI metric, instead I slightly changed my metric to fit the metric used in the initial ML-VAE paper.

The paper I sent was interesting, talking about the 3 components of disentanglement. However, these metrics are meant for situations where we don't know the correct alignment between latent components and generative factors. In our case, we do.

Instead, I realised that in the initial ML-VAE paper they directly use the "correct" disentanglement metric, trying to predict the ground-truth group factor from the group and instance variables. We call this group probing and instance probing respectively.

So if we look at the definition, we take each ground-truth factor and see how well the corresponding latent variable predicts it, as compared to all the other latent variable. We quickly see that, because of the model form, it is impossible for the wrong variable to predict the instance factor. So we just predict the group factor.

They claim that the only failure of disentanglement (outside of not maximizing the likelihood) is that the instance latent also encodes the group factor.

It would be interesting to show how this is connected to DCI disentanglement, but outside the scope of my paper at the moment.

### New experiment

The point of my paper is to propose a model which works on datasets with confounded groups.

I have a new experiment which I think really hits the nail on the head. I want to show that my model is works on confounded datasets. Can I continuously shift my dataset between confounded and unconfounded and see the performance?

I have an experiment showing the impact of changing the relative strength of the $u$ variable compared to the $v$ variable.

$$x = \lambda u + (1 - \lambda) v, ~ u, v \sim \mathcal{N} (0, 1)$$

- **What do we expect?** When $\lambda$ is too low, the group variable is non-existent, so the problem is a normal autoencoding problem. When $\lambda$ is too high, there is little confusion between the group and instance variable, so disentanglement is easy. When $\lambda=0.5$ confusion is maximal. We expect our model to perform best compared with existing methods when $\lambda=0.5$.
