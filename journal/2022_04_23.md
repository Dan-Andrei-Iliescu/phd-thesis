On one hand, I show how group-instance disentanglement can be used for translation; this is combining the group representation of one item with the instance representation for another. ## Meeting with Damon

- He asked me how I changed the paper. I said I ran experiments on font typefaces and collaborative filtering. He asked how does my method compare with COCO-FUNIT. I said that method is different in 2 different ways: It is algorithmic not probabilistic, and it conditions the group variable on the instance variable and not the other way around. There are 2 steps in my paper: first explain why the probabilistic model corresponds to FUNIT, then show empirically that it underperforms compared to my method. Damon said here is the place to use a synthetic example.
- I showed him the experiments comparing the 3 kinds of splits: by author, by item and random. He said I should try the other split as well: random holdout, validation by author. He said Tim Hillel tried this before for transportation data. He said it seems obvious to him that stratification by inherent groups helps classification. I mentioned an example of an inherent group for MNIST (the digit) by which it is disadvantageous to statify.
  - At that stage he understood my point: A generative modelling approach assumes you know which variables generate which, so it is obviously insane to stratify by digit. However, with a data-centric approach, you want the model to tell you which variables are relevant. I agreed and said 2 other examples of variables for which it is unclear if they help: the amount of ink used to draw a digit and the day the image was taken. He said mixed-effects models and Gaussian Processes have an inbuilt way of dealing with this, you just use all the covariates and compare the posterior probability. In addition those methods have a way to deal with more complex generative models than group-instance. The advantage of group-instance is that you can use an arbitrarily expressive generative model, and also you can do amortized inference of the latent effects at test time. If I can extend a VAE approach to general mixed-group models, that is a low hanging fruit and could be a chapter in my dissertation. Such a model would be more appropriate for matrix completion.
  - I mentioned another question, from causal inference: there are 2 types of domain shifts: target distribution stays the same and posterior changes (e.g. different MNIST authors), or posterior stays the same and target changes (e.g. class imbalances between domains). I am interested in the first one (I think). I think the first one is good for stratification, and the second one isn't.
