### Preparation
I want to understand how to motivate my GID paper: What is the problem, and why (intuitively) do I propose my solution? "Models with the independence assumption can't disentangle under the conditional shift scenario, therefore I propose my conditional model."

Let's define the terms:

Issues:
- How does my solution relate to how people usually deal with conditional shift? Well, the classic solution is to find an invariant representation $\phi(x)$ that satisfies $p(\phi(x) | u = 1) = p(\phi(x) | u = 2)$ ([Zhang2013Domain](https://api.semanticscholar.org/CorpusID:17069732)). In the case where $p(v | x, u=1) \neq p(v | x, u=2)$ we need a transferrable representation where $p(\phi(x) | v, u=1) = p(\phi(x) | v, u=2)$ ([Gong2016Domain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28239433/), [Li2018Deep](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-01267-0_38)). Some methods achieve this by a linear transformation of $\phi(x)$, other train a neural network to minimize this loss. I think my point here is I am not explicitly taking a solution from domain adaptation, but simply making my network expressive enough to be able to learn any solution.
- Mixed effects models also deal with this issue, but when it comes to the generative model. Pinheiro and Bates say "Without a group variable, the estimate of the instance variable will have an inflated variance."

## Collaborative filtering experiments

I'm looking for what distribution the latent variables should have for dot-product collaborative filtering. Should they be positive? What else should we add to the dot product?

Model used in the literature is $x_{ij} = \mu + b_i + b_j + \mathbf{z}_i^T \mathbf{z}_j$, where $\mu$ is a trainable parameter. I'm looking at [Koren2009Matrix](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/5197422) and [Rendle2020Neural](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3383313.3412488).
