## Abstract

We introduce a new version of Group Variational Autoencoders that can disentangle grouped data under the conditional shift scenario. Conditional shift is a property of the data generating process whereby the true conditional distribution of the instance variable changes from one group to another $p(v | x, u_i) \neq p(v | x, u_j)$. Existing methods fail to disentangle under this scenario because they produce instance-level representations contaminated with group-level information. Our method, the Context-Aware VAE (CxVAE), accounts for the variation across groups by conditioning the instance encoder on the previously inferred group representation. We validate our method empirically through the case-study of representing standardised test scores with separate student-level and school-level components. The CxVAE outperforms existing methods with respect to fitting the holdout set, learning disentangled representations, answering counterfactual questions, and predicting the ground-truth generative factors. Additionally, we demonstrate that, by decreasing the amount of conditional shift in the data-generating process, we can bridge the performance gap between the CxVAE and existing methods.

Group Variational Autoencoders are a state-of-the-art method for learning separate representations for within-group and across-group variation. These methods make the implicit assumption that the instance representations can be inferred separately from the group representations. We show that this assumption is not suited to datasets with conditional shift, where the true conditional distribution of the instance latent variable $v$ changes from one group to another (i.e. $p(v | x, u_i) \neq p(v | x, u_j)$ where $x$ is a datapoint and $u_i, u_j$ are two different groups). As a case study, we consider the task of encoding a dataset of standardised test scores into separate student-level and school-level representations, for the purpose of comparing students based on their aptitude independently of what school they attended. We show that exising methods fail to learn disentangled representations of this dataset. We introduce an extension to Group Variational Autoencoders that can learn disentangled representations under the conditional shift scenario. Our main insight is that conditional shift in this dataset causes the performance gap; by reducing the amount of conditional shift we also reduce the performance gap.
