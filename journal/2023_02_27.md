## Confounding in time-series data

[Handling time-varying confounding in observational research.](https://www.bmj.com/content/359/bmj.j4587)

You are a clinician interested in measuring the effect of testosterone treatment on the risk of myocardial infarction. The problem is that the course of the treatment is affected by a variable that also causes the outcome, namely the level of serum testosterone.

This is the setup. Patients come to the clinic and their testosterone level is measured $C_0$, and then a certain amount of testosterone replacement hormone $E_0$ is assigned. After a few weeks, you apply a second dose of testosterone $E_1$ to the patients after measuring the testosterone level again $C_1$. After a while, we measure the risk of cardiovascular disease $D$.

We propose a linear model mapping the amount of testosterone replacement hormone prescribed $E$ to the risk of cardiovascular disease $D$. The random variable $E$ is created by sampling randomly from either $E_0$ or $E_1$.
