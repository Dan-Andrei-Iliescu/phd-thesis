## Why is inpainting different form superresolution?

When we use superresolution for inpainting, the missing region will have low coherence. The pattern of missingness is different. 

How do we train if we know the pattern of missingness? The pattern of missingness is denoted by the distribution of the binary variable M. 
- One option is to always predict the absent variables given the present variables, following the distribution of M. But here again the training is dependent on sampling M.
- Another option is to condition the generative model post-hoc.

## Style-content disentanglement using group-instance

1. The content of the patch is the role that the patch plays in the image (its class).
2. The style of the patch is the distribution to which the patch belongs.

The content encoder produces a vector for each pixel. The style encoder produces one vector for the whole image. 

> But there's a problem, one pixel actually has too little information to be encoded in the style code. At the same time, half an image has too much information to need a style code. Maybe the tradeoff should be a patch size that is the square root of the image size.

The style variable should be encoded without the use of the coordinates. Should the coordinates ever be used? It doesn't seem so.

The patches define a neighbourhood. An attention map also defines a neighbourhood for each element. Each pixel must have an instance representation, and an output patch, although how we combine the output patches is tricky. One option is to take only the central pixel. Another option is to section the image. Yet another option is to somehow blend the patches.

## Sequential modifications

I want to learn the manifold of natural images by walking on the manifold from one image to another. I think this would allow the model to learn to generate images based on very few training examples. The inspiration for this is that, when I learn to draw, what I actually learn is to make errors in the direction of good drawings; i.e. as a novice, the mistakes you make when copying make the image worse. When you're an expert, the mistakes you make when copying make the image better.

My initial idea is to map normally distributed noise to a modification map which is added on top of the image. Let's say I am trying to modify image $x$.

$$x_{k+1} = \mathrm{norm} (m_k + x_k), ~ m_{k} = h_\theta (x_k, z), ~ z \sim \mathcal{N} (0, 1)$$

So there is a markov chain $p(x_{k+1} | x_k) = \mathcal{N} (x_k, 1)$

### Walking generative model

I have a dataset $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$. I express the likelihood of every datapoint in terms of every other datapoint.

$$\sum_{i=1}^N \log p(x) = \sum_{i=1}^N \log \mathbb{E}_{p(z | x') p(x')} p(x | z, x')$$

I choose $p(z | x')$ instead of $p(z)$. We perform variational inference by replacing $p(z | x')$ with $q(z | x, x')$
