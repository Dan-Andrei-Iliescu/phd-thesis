# Writing in the sciences

## 1.1 Introduction
1. Good writing communicates an idea clearly and effectively. It takes having something to say, and clear thinking.
2. Good writing is elegant and stylish. Takes time, revision and good editing!

What makes a good writer:
- Knowing what you want to say
- Logical and clear thinking
- Rules of style

How to be a better writer
- Read, pay attention and imitate.
- Write in a journal.
- Let go of bad writing habits.
- Talk about your research before writing about it.
- Write to engage your reader - try not to bore them.
- Stop waiting for inspiration.
- Accept that writing is hard for everyone.
- Revise and revise.
- Learn how to not be too attached to my words.
- Find a good editor.
- Take risks

## 1.2 What not to do
A classic phenomenon in academic writing is to turn active verbs into nouns. Verbs speed up a sentence, whereas nouns slow it down.
Complex ideas don't require complex language.
Reading your paper should be enjoyable.

## 1.3 Principles of effective writing
- Use specific terms
- Avoid acronyms
- Avoid the passive voice
- Minimize the number of words between your subject and your verb (burying the verb)

## 1.4 Cutting the clutter
If you catch yourself reaching for a thesaurus to find a synonym for a word, ask yourself if you even need that second instance of the word at all. Remove inherent repetitions. Be vigilant and ruthless. Convey the same idea with more power.

Common clutter: preambles, vague terms, long phrases that could be short, unnecessary jargons and acronyms, extra repetitions, adverbs

## 1.5 Tricks to cut the clutter
- Cut negatives.
- Cut "there are/there is".
- Cut prepositions.

## 2.1 Active-Passive voice

Ask the question "Who does what to whom?". Advantages of the active voice:
1. Shows who is responsible for the action.
2. Shortens the sentence.
3. Makes it less ambiguous. Moving from passive to active reveals missing information.

Passive voice may be appropriate for the methods section, where what was done is more important than who did it.

## 2.4 Strong verbs
Replace an adverb with a stronger, more precise verb. Use "to be" verbs sparingly. Don't kill verbs by turning them into nouns. Don't bury the main verb because the reader is waiting for the verb.

## 2.6 Grammar tips
- *Data* is plural.
- *Affect* is generally a verb, *effect* is generally a noun.
- *Compare to* points out similarities between different things, *compare with* shows differences between similar things.
- *That* introduces a restrictive clause (doesn't have a comma), *which* introduces a nonrestrictive clause (has a comma).
